When prices rise across the board, it is called inflation. It happens when people spend faster than the economy can make things, when a key cost like energy goes up, or when money and credit grow faster than output. Expecting higher prices can help cause them too. Most central banks try to keep inflation low and steady, often about 2% a year, rather than at zero. So a little rise each year is normal; the problem is when it runs high.
