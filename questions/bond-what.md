A bond is a loan you can buy and sell. You lend money to a government or a company, it pays you interest along the way, and it pays the money back on a set date. A government that borrows in its own currency can always create money to pay, so its bonds are usually seen as among the safest promises there are. The catch is that if interest rates rise, older bonds that pay less are worth less if you sell early. Rising prices can also shrink what your money buys when it comes back.
