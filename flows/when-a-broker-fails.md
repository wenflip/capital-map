The bottom rung of the [[failure-modes|failure ladder]], step by step: the firm that holds your shares fails, and each protection is tested in turn.

> **Key rule:** a broker's failure is usually survivable if your assets were kept apart from the firm's own and the records are right. The losses come where they weren't, and the cost of waiting comes either way.

| Step | Page | What happens | What you hold | What can go wrong | Where it happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The broker runs into trouble | [[brokers]] | Trading losses, fraud or a rush of withdrawals leave the firm unable to meet what it owes | A claim on the broker: your cash, and shares held in its name for you | Trouble is often hidden until the last days | MF Global collapsed on 31 October 2011 with more than $1.6 billion of customer money missing that should have been kept apart |
| Accounts are frozen and an administrator takes over | [[broker-failure]] | A court appoints a trustee or administrator, and customer accounts stop moving | The same claim, now frozen | You can't sell or move anything, however far markets move | Madoff's firm was revealed as a fraud on 11 December 2008, and a court-appointed trustee took over |
| Segregated client assets are found and returned | [[custody]] | Assets held apart from the firm's own are transferred to another broker or handed back | Your shares again, if they were kept apart and the records match | Missing records, or customer assets mixed with the firm's own | — |
| A protection scheme covers missing assets, up to its limit | [[broker-failure]] | Where customer assets are missing, the scheme pays up to its limit | A claim on the scheme | It replaces missing assets, not lost value; it has limits; many crypto platforms belong to no scheme | US: SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 of cash |
| Any shortfall is claimed like any other creditor | [[claims-and-liabilities]] | Whatever is still missing joins the queue of the firm's creditors | A creditor's claim, paid if and when assets are recovered | Years of waiting; claims fixed at their value on the day of failure | MF Global customers were repaid in full only in 2014. FTX claims were valued in dollars at the November 2022 bankruptcy, near a market low |

## What to notice
- **Segregation does most of the work.** The scheme and the creditors' queue only matter for what was *not* kept apart. See [[custody]].
- **Time is a cost even when you're repaid in full.** A frozen account can't be sold in a falling market or used for anything else.
- **Repaid in full can still mean behind.** A claim fixed in dollars on the day of failure misses any later rise in the assets you held. See [[custodial-crypto]].

*Sources: InvestmentNews, MF Global starts final repayments of 100% of customer claims (2014); SIPC, Madoff trustee distribution (2018); SIPC, What SIPC Protects; The Block, FTX major repayments to begin May 30 (2025).*
