*Where money comes from* starts with a central bank buying a bond. This flow follows what happens when it buys on a huge scale (quantitative easing, QE), and when it lets those bonds run off (quantitative tightening, QT). QE is a tool of [[monetary-policy]] for when the policy rate is already near zero.

> **Key rule:** QE doesn't hand money to the public. It swaps bonds for reserves, and its effects come from what that swap does to yields, prices and the money investors have to place.

| The sellers' banks hold more reserves, and their customers more deposits | [[bank-deposits]] | When a pension fund or insurer sells, its bank receives the reserves and the fund receives a deposit | Banks hold reserves; investors hold deposits instead of bonds | More money exists, but it sits as deposits; nothing forces it to be spent | — |
| Bond prices rise and yields fall | [[bond-pricing]] | A buyer that doesn't need a profit bids for the same bonds as everyone else | Holders of existing bonds gain | Prices stop telling you what private buyers alone would pay | — |
| Long-term rates fall and the yield curve flattens | [[yield-curve]] | Mortgages and company borrowing get cheaper | Borrowers lock in low rates for years | Savers earn less, and debts pile up while borrowing is cheap | US, 25 November 2008: the Fed announced it would buy up to $500 billion of mortgage-backed securities and $100 billion of housing-agency debt, to cut the cost of home loans |

## What to notice
- **QE is a swap, not a gift.** The public ends up with deposits instead of bonds. The central bank's [[central-bank-money|balance sheet]] grows, and so does the money in the system, but the effect on prices runs through yields and asset prices.
- **The same bond, both ways.** Buying pushes yields down; letting bonds mature, or selling them, pushes them up. See [[duration]] for why long bonds move the most.
- **QT has a floor.** Banks need enough reserves to settle with each other. Shrink them too far and short-term rates break away from the policy rate, which is one reason central banks stop QT before the balance sheet is back to where it started.
- **QE and QT sit inside crises too.** In 2022 the Bank of England paused its sales and bought bonds again within days, because a market for long-dated bonds stopped working. See [[market-failure]].
