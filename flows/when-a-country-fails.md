*When a bank fails* tests the backstops behind one bank. This flow climbs higher up the [[failure-modes|failure ladder]]: the government itself runs out of ways to pay. Real crises don't always follow every step, or in this order, but these links recur.

> **Key rule:** at the top of the ladder, failures arrive together. Inflation, controls, currency loss and default often hit the same savers at once.

| Step | Page | Who acts | Whose liability | What can break | Where it happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deficits are funded by the central bank | [[central-bank-money]] | Government and central bank | The central bank's money | Trust, if printing outruns the economy | — |
| Inflation rises while interest rates are held below it | [[financial-repression]] | Central bank and regulators | Government bonds and deposits | Savers' real wealth, slowly | US and UK, 1945–1980 |
| Money tries to leave; controls go up | [[capital-controls]] | Government | Deposits inside the country | Access to your own money | Iceland 2008–17 · Greece 2015–19 |
| Deposits are frozen or converted | [[bank-deposits]] | Banks, on government orders | The banks | Deposits' value and access | Argentina, 2001 |
| The currency collapses or is redenominated | [[currency-failure]] | Markets, then the government | The central bank | Everything priced in the currency | Germany 1923 · Zimbabwe 2008 |
| The government defaults or restructures | [[sovereign-default]] | Government, IMF, creditors | The government | Bondholders' claims | Russia 1998 · Greece 2012 · Sri Lanka 2022 |
| Banks holding government bonds fail | [[bank-sovereign-loop]] | Banks and the state | Both, at once | The rescue, if the rescuer is the one in trouble | Euro area, from 2010 |

## What to notice
- **Each step shifts losses onto savers by a different route:** inflation, controls, conversion, devaluation, haircut.
- **Domestic "safe" assets are hit hardest:** cash, deposits and government bonds all depend on the same government.
- **Stores outside the country's system,** such as foreign assets, real assets and [[human-capital]] that can move, are harder to reach. That's why the [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]] asks how visible each store is to the state.
