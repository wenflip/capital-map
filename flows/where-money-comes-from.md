One dollar followed from the base of the system to your account and back. Each step adds a layer of promise on top of the one below.

> **Key rule:** each step up the chain is a new promise, made by a new party. Each one is a place the chain can break.

| Step | Page | Who creates it | Whose liability | What can break |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central bank buys a bond | [[central-bank-money]] | Central bank | Central bank (reserves) | Trust in the currency; inflation |
| Bank lends to a borrower | [[money-creation]] | Commercial bank | Bank (new deposit) | Bad loans; bank failure |
| Borrower pays you | [[bank-deposits]] | — (deposit moves) | Your bank | Bank run; freeze |
| Your bank settles with theirs | [[central-bank-money]] | — (reserves move) | Central bank | — (final settlement) |
| You withdraw notes | [[cash-and-fiat]] | Central bank | Central bank (notes) | Theft; inflation |
| Loan is repaid | [[money-creation]] | — | — (deposit destroyed) | — |

## What to notice
- Most of the money you see was created by a **bank loan**, not printed by the government.
- Final settlement between banks always happens in **central bank money**, the base layer of the [[hierarchy-of-money|hierarchy of money]].
- Repaying loans **destroys** money, which is why credit busts shrink the money supply.
