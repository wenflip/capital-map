**The Capital Map** is a free, independent reference to money, markets and assets: cash, bonds, shares, real estate, gold and crypto, and the skills and relationships that sit underneath them all.

It lays the whole system out as one connected map. Each concept has one page. Every page can be found on the map, traced through the flows that use it, and checked against the question that sorts all money and claims: *whose liability is it, and what is it a claim on?*

**Who it's for:** anyone who wants to understand how money and investing actually work, from first principles, without a sales pitch.

## What it is
- **One system, several lenses.** The tree sorts things by what they *are*. The [[cartography-of-wealth]] sorts stores of wealth by what can *kill* them. Flows show how money *moves*. All three point at the same pages.
- **Concepts, not tips.** Each page explains what something is, why it exists, how it works and what goes wrong.
- **Built to last.** Pages avoid prices, dates and forecasts, so they don't go out of date.

## What it isn't
- **Not financial advice.** Nothing here tells you what to buy, sell or hold. It explains how things work so you can judge for yourself.
- **Not a news or price site.** For live data, see [[data-sources]].

## Ways in
- **Map** — the system as a tree. Start at [[capital-system]] and expand any branch.
- **Flows** — money followed step by step, such as where a dollar comes from.
- **Index and search** — every page. Press `/` to search, `H` to go home. Ctrl+click any link to open it in a new tab.
- **Depth** — *Core* for the essentials, *Everything* to add the advanced layer (marked ◆) and the "Going deeper" sections.
- **Welcome guide** — the same ways in, on one card. It opens on a first visit; reopen it at the foot of this page.

The Capital Map is a sibling of The Supply Map and shares its engine.

## Accuracy and corrections
Pages are checked against reliable sources and reviewed for accuracy, and worked examples use fictional figures. Mistakes still happen, and some topics are simplified so the main idea is clear. Treat every page as a starting point, not the final word, and check anything you plan to act on.
