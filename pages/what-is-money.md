## What it is
Money is whatever people accept as final payment. Economists describe it by the jobs it does:

| Job | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Medium of exchange** | Accepted in payment for goods, services and debts | Paying for groceries |
| **Unit of account** | The measure prices and debts are written in | A loan of 10,000 dollars |
| **Store of value** | Keeps purchasing power over time | Savings for next year |

## Why it matters
Different assets are good at different jobs:
- **Cash** is an excellent medium of exchange and unit of account, but a weak store of value over long periods because of [[inflation]].
- **Gold** has been a strong long-run store of value, but is awkward to pay with.
- **Bitcoin** is argued by supporters to be a store of value; it is rarely used as a unit of account.
- **Memecoins** do none of the three jobs reliably.

## How to think about it
Money is not a thing so much as a **promise that everyone accepts**. The question that sorts one money from another is whose promise it is, and what stands behind it. See [[claims-and-liabilities]] and [[hierarchy-of-money]].

> **Key rule:** something can be good money for spending and poor money for saving. Judge each job separately.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why is a currency that loses value every year still used as money?

**A:** Because it does the other two jobs very well. Debts, wages and taxes are written in it, and everyone accepts it in payment. Its weakness is only as a long-term store of value.
