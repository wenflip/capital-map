## What it is
A mental model, not measured data. It maps stores of wealth on two axes:
- **Inside you → outside you:** how embodied the store is.
- **Hidden → recorded by authority:** how visible it is to the state.

Each store gets one **killer**: the single failure most likely to send it to zero.

> **Key rule:** risk can't be destroyed, only moved and reshaped. No asset is safe — only a spread across failures that don't strike together.

## The three tiers

| Tier | Protected from… | Exposed to… |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Inside you** | Freezing, theft, inflation | Dying with you; barely transfers to heirs |
| **2 · Private / bearer** | The state | Theft and your own mistakes, with no one to call |
| **3 · Legible / state-visible** | Theft, via courts, police and insurance | The state itself |

## What the map shows
- **Stores cluster on a diagonal.** What is embodied tends to be hidden; what is external tends to be legible. Almost nothing is both inside you and visible to the state.
- **Moving down the diagonal trades one risk for another:** theft and self-error for state and counterparty risk. You never escape both.
- **The enemy is correlation, not risk.** Spread across the axes so that no single disaster clears the board. See [[diversification]] and [[correlation]].
- **Tier 1 is the root store.** Skills and relationships are the only stores that can rebuild the others from zero.

## The link to claims
The visibility axis lines up closely with [[claims-and-liabilities]]. Claims (deposits, bonds, shares) are someone's liability, so they must be recorded. Things that are nobody's liability (gold, bearer crypto, skills) can be held privately. Real estate is the main exception: it is nobody's liability, but a title registry makes it fully visible.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Physical gold and a gold fund both track the price of gold. Why do they sit in different tiers?

**A:** Physical gold is a bearer asset: private, but exposed to theft. A gold fund is a recorded claim on the fund and your broker: legible and protected by the state, but exposed to counterparty and state risk. Same price, different killers.

**Q:** According to the model, why is spreading money across many shares not enough on its own?

**A:** Because every share sits in the same tier with the same kinds of killer: custodian, market and state risk. The model's point is to spread across failures that don't strike together, which means across tiers, not just within one.

## Limits of the model
- Placements are **judgments**. Reasonable people would move some dots.
- It is **one lens** among several. It emphasises seizure, theft and failure; it says little about expected returns.
- Some forms break the quadrants and are worth studying for that reason: [[custodial-crypto]] (crypto held as a legible claim on an exchange), gold funds (paper gold, a claim), and [[stablecoins]] (a private dollar promise).
