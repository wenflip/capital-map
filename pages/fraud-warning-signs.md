## What it is
Fraud warning signs are patterns that appear repeatedly when investment returns are fabricated or when new money is used to pay earlier investors. They are not a complete list, and none of them proves fraud on its own. Together they raise the chance that the reported results are not what they seem.

## Why it matters
Once money is handed over, recovering it is slow and incomplete. Recognising the patterns before the transfer is cheaper than relying on later legal remedies. The same patterns have appeared in many cases; the assets and the stories change.

## How it works
Common patterns include:
- Returns that never show a losing month, or that move in a narrow band month after month.
- Returns that are high relative to the risk the stated method should involve.
- A strategy that cannot be explained or independently verified.
- Pressure to decide quickly, or an offer of “exclusive” access.
- No independent custodian — the manager holds the assets.
- A small or unknown auditor.
- Difficulty withdrawing money.
- Payments to existing investors that depend on money from new investors.

**Worked example (fictional figures):** a fund reports a gain of 1% every month and never a loss. Over twelve months that is 1.01^12 ≈ **1.1268**, or about 12.7%. The level is high but not impossible. The absence of any losing month is the stronger signal. Real strategies that target that return normally show variation; a path with zero variation is difficult to produce without either extraordinary skill or fabricated numbers. See [[volatility]] and [[sharpe-ratio]].

The Madoff case (2008) showed several of these patterns at once: reported results that were both high and unusually smooth, a strategy that was hard to reconcile with the volumes claimed, and assets that turned out not to be held as stated. By 2018 the trustee had recovered about $13.3 billion. See [[broker-failure]].

## What goes wrong
- **Treating one smooth year as proof.** Short periods can be quiet by chance.
- **Assuming a well-known name removes the risk.** Reputation has not prevented large frauds.
- **Focusing only on the return level.** The smoothness and the custody arrangements are often more informative than the headline percentage.

> **Key rule:** if you cannot see who holds the money and why the returns are what they are, do not hand it over.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A strategy reports 10% a year with almost no monthly variation. Why is the lack of variation more informative than the 10%?

**A:** Many legitimate strategies can produce 10% in some periods. Very few can produce it with almost no losing months, because real markets and real methods generate variation. The smoothness is harder to fake by chance than the level.

**Q:** The manager says the assets are held at a well-known bank, but statements come only from the manager. What is missing?

**A:** Independent confirmation from the custodian. Statements prepared by the manager do not show that the assets exist outside the manager’s control.
