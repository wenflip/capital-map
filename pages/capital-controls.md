## What fails
Your **access** to your own money. In a crisis a government can cap cash withdrawals, block transfers abroad, or freeze or convert deposits. The money still exists on paper, but you can't move or use it freely.

## Why governments do it
To stop a run: if everyone tries to withdraw or send money abroad at once, banks and the currency can collapse. Controls buy time. Since 2012 the IMF has accepted that controls can be appropriate in some crises.

## The backstop
For this rung there usually isn't one. The state is the actor, not the protector. What softens controls is their design: allowances for essentials, exemptions for trade, and a clear plan to lift them.

## Where the backstop runs out
- **They last longer than promised.** Temporary controls have often stayed for years.
- **They come with other losses.** Controls often arrive together with bank losses, currency conversion or devaluation.
- **They catch everyone,** including depositors at healthy banks.

## Cases
| Country | Imposed | Lifted | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argentina | December 2001 | Eased over 2002 | Withdrawals capped at 250 pesos a week; dollar deposits later converted to pesos at 1.4 to the dollar as the market rate rose towards 4 |
| Iceland | 2008 | March 2017 | Controls on moving money out lasted more than eight years |
| Cyprus | March 2013 | April 2015 | Imposed alongside the bail-in of large deposits |
| Greece | June 2015 | 1 September 2019 | Banks shut for three weeks; withdrawals capped at €60 a day; controls eased over four years |

## What it kills
[[bank-deposits]] and [[cash-and-fiat]] held inside the country, and any [[shares]] or [[bonds]] you can't sell or move abroad. Physical [[gold]], [[self-custody]] crypto and assets already held abroad are harder to trap, though not beyond the law.

> **Key rule:** "temporary" controls are measured in years. Money you might need in a crisis is only safe if it can't all be frozen by one decision.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Greece's capital controls began in June 2015. How long until they were fully lifted?

**A:** Just over four years, until 1 September 2019.

**Q:** Why are capital controls described as a rung with no backstop?

**A:** Because the government imposing them is the same body that would normally protect you. The only protection is how the controls are designed and how quickly they're lifted.
