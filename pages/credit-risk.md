## What it is
**Credit risk** is the risk that a borrower misses payments or doesn't repay in full. It's the reason a company's bond pays more than its government's.

## Why it matters
Credit risk is the killer for [[bonds]] on the [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]]: a bond is someone else's promise to pay. The higher a bond's yield compared with government debt, the more doubt the market has about that promise.

## How it works
**Ratings.** Rating agencies grade borrowers with letter scales, from the strongest (AAA, or Aaa on one agency's scale) downwards.
- **Investment grade:** BBB− (Baa3) and above.
- **High yield** (or "junk"): below that. Many funds aren't allowed to hold it, so a downgrade across the line can force selling.

**Spreads.** The **credit spread** is the extra yield over a comparable government bond.

**Worked example (fictional figures):** a company bond yields 6.5% when a government bond of the same maturity yields 4.5%. The spread is 2 percentage points, usually written **200 basis points** (one basis point is 0.01%).

**Recovery.** Default rarely means getting nothing back. Bondholders recover part of their money through a restructuring or bankruptcy; how much depends on the company's assets and where the bond sits in the queue of creditors. See [[claims-and-liabilities]].

## What goes wrong
- **Ratings lag.** Agencies often downgrade after markets have already lost confidence. Before 2008, many bonds built from risky mortgages were rated AAA and later defaulted.
- **Spreads jump together.** In a crisis, spreads widen for almost all risky borrowers at once, so diversifying across companies helps less than it seems. See [[correlation]] and [[credit-cycle]].
- **Protection can fail too.** A credit default swap pays out if a borrower defaults, but only if the seller of the protection can still pay. See [[swaps]].
- **Governments can default too**, especially on debt in a currency they can't create. See [[bank-sovereign-loop]].

> **Key rule:** extra yield is payment for extra risk. Ask what the spread is paying you to worry about.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A bond is downgraded from BBB− to BB+. Why might its price fall more than the small step suggests?

**A:** Because it crosses from investment grade to high yield. Funds that may hold only investment-grade bonds may have to sell, all at once.
