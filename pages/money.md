## What it is
The base layer of the whole system. Every other asset is priced in money, borrowed in money and sold for money, so how money works shapes everything above it.

This branch is about the machinery, and covers two linked ideas:
- **Money:** what it is, and why it comes in layers, each one a promise to pay in the layer below.
- **Liquidity:** how much money is moving through the financial system at any time, and why prices across many markets follow it.

The forms of money people actually keep (banknotes, bank deposits and gold) have their own branch, [[money-you-hold|Money You Hold]], with the other things you can own.

## Why it exists
Without a shared way to pay and keep score, every trade would need a direct swap of goods. Money solves that, but not all money is equal. A banknote, a bank deposit and a stablecoin can all be "a dollar", yet they are different promises from different issuers. In calm markets the differences are invisible. In a crisis they decide who gets paid.

## What goes wrong
Each layer fails in its own way:

| Layer | Typical failure |
|---|---|
| Gold | Theft, confiscation, long periods with no real gain |
| Central bank money and cash | Inflation; in the worst cases, a currency crisis |
| Bank deposits | Bank runs, freezes, losses above the insurance limit |
| Private and offshore dollars | De-pegs and dollar shortages when the issuer or bank can't pay |

When trust drops, money moves **down** the hierarchy towards the base. That move is what a bank run, a stablecoin de-peg and a "dash for cash" have in common.

> **Key rule:** always ask whose promise you are holding. The further a form of money sits from the base, the more ways it can fail.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A bank deposit and a banknote both say "one dollar". What is the difference between them?

**A:** A banknote is the central bank's liability. A deposit is your bank's liability: a promise to pay you central bank money. If the bank fails, the deposit is at risk above the insurance limit; the note is not.

**Q:** Why do panics push money *down* the hierarchy rather than up?

**A:** Because people sell promises they doubt (upper layers) to get money that depends on fewer parties staying solvent (lower layers). The base is the safest place to wait.
