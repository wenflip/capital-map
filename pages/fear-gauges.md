## What it is
A **fear gauge** is a price that rises when investors are nervous. Two are watched most:
- **Expected volatility,** worked out from the prices of options. When people pay more for protection against big moves, it rises. In the US, the best-known measure is the **VIX**.
- **Credit spreads,** the extra interest that riskier borrowers pay over safe government borrowing. When lenders worry about defaults, spreads widen. See [[credit-risk]].

## Why it matters
Price charts show what has happened. Fear gauges show what investors are paying to protect against what might happen next. They are one of the clearest windows into the market's mood, and into whether it has shifted from risk-on to risk-off. See [[market-regimes]].

## How it works
**The VIX, as an example.** Cboe first published the VIX in 1993. It measures the market's expectation of how much the S&P 500, a US share index, will move over the next 30 days, worked out from the prices of options on that index. See [[options]].
- In calm markets it tends to sit low; in panics it jumps.
- It closed at a record **82.69 on 16 March 2020**, above its previous peak of 80.74 in November 2008.
- Other markets have similar measures built the same way.

**Reading the number.** The VIX is an expected yearly range. A common rough conversion to a daily move divides by about 16. Fictional example: a reading of 32 means traders expect daily moves of roughly 32 ÷ 16 = **2%**. A reading of 16 suggests about 1% a day. It's a rule of thumb, not a forecast.

**Credit spreads tell the same story in the debt market.** When spreads widen quickly, lenders are demanding more to take risk, and borrowing gets harder for everyone. See [[credit-cycle]].

## What goes wrong
- **A gauge isn't a forecast.** It shows how nervous investors are now, not what will happen.
- **High readings don't reliably mark the bottom.** They often come near the worst of a fall, but fear can stay high for months.
- **Low readings can mean complacency.** Long periods of calm encourage borrowing and crowded positions. See [[stability-breeds-instability]].

> **Key rule:** fear gauges measure the price of protection. When protection is cheap, that is the time people forget they need it.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Using the rough rule, what daily move does a VIX reading of 48 suggest?

**A:** About 48 ÷ 16 = **3%** a day, up or down.

**Q:** Why can a very low fear gauge be a warning rather than a comfort?

**A:** Because calm encourages people to borrow more and protect less. When something finally goes wrong, more of them are caught unprepared at once.
