## What it is
The same fund can be held in different kinds of **account**, and the account decides how it is taxed, when you can take money out, and sometimes how much you can add. There are three broad kinds:

| Kind | Tax | Access | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary (taxable)** | Income and gains taxed as normal | Any time | A standard brokerage account |
| **Tax-advantaged savings** | Growth free of tax, or tax deferred | Usually any time, within limits | UK ISA, Canadian TFSA |
| **Retirement** | Tax relief going in or coming out | Locked until a set age | US 401(k) and IRA, UK workplace pensions |

Names, limits and rules vary by country and change over time.

## Why it exists
Governments give tax breaks to encourage saving, especially for retirement. In return, they set limits on how much can go in and, for retirement accounts, when it can come out.

## How it works
**Two ways retirement accounts give tax relief:**
- **Relief going in:** contributions reduce your taxable income now; withdrawals are taxed later.
- **Relief coming out:** contributions are made from taxed income; growth and withdrawals are tax-free.

**Employer contributions.** Many workplace retirement plans add money when you contribute, up to a limit. That is an immediate return before any investment growth, and missing it is one of the costliest mistakes. See [[common-mistakes]].

**A common order of filling accounts:** first any employer-matched retirement contribution, then tax-advantaged accounts up to their limits, then ordinary accounts. It's a rule of thumb, not advice for your situation.

## What goes wrong
- **Locking up money you'll need.** Retirement accounts can charge penalties for early withdrawal.
- **Losing allowances.** Some yearly limits can't be carried forward; unused room is gone.
- **Holding the wrong things in the wrong place.** Assets that produce a lot of taxed income often belong in sheltered accounts.
- **Rule changes** that alter the value of an account after you've chosen it.

> **Key rule:** the account is part of the investment. Use the tax breaks you're entitled to, but keep enough in accessible savings for emergencies.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Your employer adds 50 for every 100 you put in its retirement plan, up to a limit. What return have you made before any investment growth?

**A:** 50%. Every 100 you contribute immediately becomes 150.
