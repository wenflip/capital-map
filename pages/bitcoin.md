## What it is
**Bitcoin** is the first blockchain and its native coin, launched in 2009. It was designed to be digital cash that doesn't depend on a bank or a government.

## Why it matters
Bitcoin is the reference example of a digital asset that is **nobody's liability**. Its supporters compare it to digital gold: scarce, durable and hard to seize when held privately. Critics point out that it pays no income and that its price has been very volatile.

## How it works
- **Fixed supply.** The rules allow no more than 21 million bitcoin.
- **Halvings.** New coins are paid to miners with each block, and that reward halves roughly every four years, so new supply keeps shrinking.
- **Proof of work.** Miners compete to add blocks about every ten minutes. See [[consensus]].
- **Deliberately simple.** Bitcoin's scripting is limited on purpose; most complex applications live on other chains. See [[smart-contract-platforms]].

**Where it sits on the Cartography of Wealth:** self-held bitcoin is a Tier 2 bearer asset (private, exposed to theft and mistakes). Bitcoin held on an exchange or in a fund is a Tier 3 claim on that firm.

## What goes wrong
- **Lost keys** mean lost coins. See [[keys-and-wallets]].
- **Custodian failure** turns your coins into a claim in a bankruptcy. See [[custodial-crypto]].
- **Volatility:** large falls have happened repeatedly. See [[drawdown]].
- **Security budget:** as block rewards shrink, fees must pay more of the cost of securing the network over time.

> **Key rule:** bitcoin's protocol has proved hard to break; most losses come from how people hold it.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why does bitcoin's new supply keep falling?

**A:** Because the reward for each new block halves roughly every four years, until the 21 million limit is reached.
