## What it is
A metal that has been used as money for thousands of years. Physical gold is a **bearer asset**: whoever holds it owns it, and no one owes it to you. Central banks still hold large gold reserves alongside their own currencies.

## Why it exists as money
- **Scarce and hard to produce.** New supply each year is small next to the existing stock.
- **Durable and uniform.** It doesn't rot or rust, and one ounce is the same as another.
- **Nobody's liability.** It can't default, and it doesn't depend on any issuer staying solvent.

## How you can hold it

| Form | What you own | Whose risk? |
|---|---|---|
| Coins or bars in your possession | The metal | Yours: theft, loss, storage |
| Allocated vault storage | Specific bars held for you | The vault operator's honesty and safety |
| Gold ETF or fund | Shares in a fund that holds gold | The fund and your broker: this is a claim, not the metal |
| Gold futures | A contract | The exchange and your counterparty |

Physical gold sits in **Tier 2** of the [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]]: private and beyond easy reach of the state, but exposed to theft and your own mistakes. Paper forms (ETFs, futures) are claims and sit with other legible assets in **Tier 3**.

## What goes wrong
- **Theft.** A bearer asset has no recourse desk.
- **Confiscation.** In 1933 the United States required citizens to hand in most private gold holdings.
- **No yield.** Gold pays nothing while you hold it, and storage costs money.
- **Long flat periods.** Gold has gone many years without rising in real terms.

> **Key rule:** "gold" on a broker statement and gold in a safe are different assets with different killers.
