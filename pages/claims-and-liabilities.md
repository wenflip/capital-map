## What it is
A **claim** is a right to receive something from someone. The other side of every claim is a **liability**: the duty to pay. Your bank deposit is your claim and the bank's liability. A bond is your claim and the borrower's liability.

Some assets are **nobody's liability**. Gold, land, bearer crypto and your own skills don't depend on anyone keeping a promise.

## Why it matters
"Is it backed?" is a weak test. Almost no modern money can be redeemed for anything. The stronger test is two questions:
1. **Whose liability is it?** That tells you who has to stay solvent and honest for it to keep its value.
2. **What is it a claim on?** That tells you what you actually get if it pays.

## How it sorts the assets

| Asset | Whose liability? | Claim on |
|---|---|---|
| [[gold]] | Nobody's | — (it is the asset) |
| [[central-bank-money]] | The central bank's | Nothing redeemable |
| [[bank-deposits]] | Your bank's | Central bank money |
| [[stablecoins]] | The issuing company's | Dollars held in reserve |
| [[bonds]] | A government or company | Future money |
| [[shares]] | The company (as the residual owner) | Future profits, after everyone else |
| [[real-estate]] | Nobody's | Use and rent |
| [[bitcoin]] | Nobody's | — |
| [[memecoins]] | Nobody's | — (attention only) |

## The link to visibility
Claims have to be recorded, because both sides need proof of who owes whom. That is why claims are visible to the state. Things that are nobody's liability *can* be held privately. This is the same divide the [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]] draws between legible and bearer assets.

> **Key rule:** a claim is only as good as the party who owes it. Being nobody's liability removes that risk, but replaces it with others: theft, loss and no one to call.
