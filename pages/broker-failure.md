## What fails
Your **broker**, exchange or custodian: the firm that holds your shares, funds, cash or crypto on your behalf. What it promised is that your assets are yours, kept apart from its own, and returned on request. See [[brokers]] and [[custody]].

## The backstop
- **Segregation.** Client assets should be held separately from the firm's own, so the firm's creditors can't claim them.
- **Records and audits** that show which customer owns what.
- **Investor protection schemes.** In the US, SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 of cash, when customer assets are missing after a broker fails. Other countries have their own schemes and limits.

## Where the backstop runs out
- **Fraud and missing assets.** Segregation only works if the firm actually did it.
- **The limits.** Losses above the scheme's cap are shared with other creditors.
- **Not every asset or firm is covered.** SIPC doesn't cover a fall in prices, and many crypto platforms aren't members of any scheme.
- **Time.** Even when customers are repaid in full, their money can be frozen for months or years.

## Cases
- **Madoff, 2008.** On 11 December 2008 Bernard Madoff's investment firm was revealed as a fraud: customer statements showed holdings that didn't exist. By 2018 the court-appointed trustee had recovered about $13.3 billion and returned about two-thirds of allowed customer claims.
- **MF Global, 2011.** The broker collapsed on 31 October 2011 with more than $1.6 billion of customer money missing that should have been segregated. Customers were made 100% whole only in 2014, two and a half years later.
- **FTX, 2022.** Customer funds were misused. Claims were valued in dollars at the bankruptcy date, near a market low, so customers repaid in full still missed the later rise. See [[custodial-crypto]].

## What it kills
Everything held through the firm: [[shares]], [[bonds]], [[funds-and-etfs|funds]] and cash at a broker, and [[custodial-crypto]] on an exchange. It doesn't touch [[self-custody]] crypto, physical [[gold]] or [[real-estate]] you hold title to.

> **Key rule:** a protection scheme repays missing assets, not market losses, and only up to its limit. Spread large holdings across firms, and know which scheme, if any, covers each one.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Your US broker fails and your shares have also fallen 20%. What does SIPC cover?

**A:** Only shares or cash that are missing because of the failure, up to the limits. It doesn't make up the 20% fall in price.

**Q:** MF Global's customers were repaid in full. Why is that case still a warning?

**A:** Because segregation had failed, and it took two and a half years. Being repaid eventually isn't the same as having access to your money when you need it.
