## What it is
A **broker** is the firm that opens your investment account, takes your orders and sends them to the market, and usually holds your shares and cash on your behalf. Banks, specialist investment platforms, trading apps and full-service advisers can all act as brokers.

## Why it exists
Individuals can't trade directly on an exchange; only member firms can. A broker gives you access, keeps records, handles dividends and tax paperwork, and arranges custody.

## How it works
- **Routing orders.** The broker sends your order to an exchange or another venue. In some markets, such as the US, brokers can be paid by trading firms to send orders to them (**payment for order flow**), which is how some apps offer "zero commission" trading.
- **Holding assets.** Your shares are usually registered in the broker's name for your benefit, in a **nominee** or **omnibus** account. They should be kept separate from the broker's own money. [[custody|Custody]] draws the whole chain, from you to the company. See [[custody]].
- **Margin accounts.** Brokers lend money against your holdings, which adds [[leverage]] and the risk of a margin call.
- **Protection schemes.** Many countries cover customers if a broker fails and assets are missing, up to a limit. In the US, SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 of cash. None of these schemes cover a fall in prices.

## What goes wrong
- **Restrictions in a frenzy.** In January 2021, Robinhood limited buying of GameStop after its clearing house demanded a deposit of about $3 billion, later cut to about $1.4 billion.
- **Outages** when markets are busiest.
- **Missing customer assets** if a broker misuses them, which is what protection schemes are for.
- **Hidden costs:** wide spreads, currency charges and account fees can matter more than the headline commission.

> **Key rule:** pick a broker for how it holds your assets and what protects them, not only for low fees.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A trading app charges no commission. How else might it be paid?

**A:** Through payment for order flow, the spread on trades, interest on customers' cash, margin lending, or currency and account fees.
