## What it is
The common picture is that banks take in deposits and lend them out. In practice it mostly works the other way round: **when a bank makes a loan, it creates a new deposit** in the borrower's account. Money is created by the stroke of a pen.

The Bank of England set this out plainly in a widely cited bulletin, *Money creation in the modern economy*.

## How it works
1. A bank approves a loan of 100.
2. It records a new asset (the loan) and a new liability (a deposit of 100 in the borrower's account).
3. The borrower spends the deposit, which moves to other banks, but it still exists as a deposit somewhere.
4. When the loan is repaid, the deposit is destroyed.

## What limits it
- **Demand for loans** from creditworthy borrowers.
- **Profitability:** the bank must earn more on the loan than it pays to fund it.
- **Regulation:** capital and liquidity rules.
- **Central bank policy rates**, which set the cost of funding.

> **Key rule:** lending creates deposits, and repaying loans destroys them. Credit booms expand the money supply; credit busts shrink it.
