## What it is
**Leverage** means controlling a position larger than your own money, by borrowing or by using derivatives that need only a deposit. It multiplies gains and losses alike.

## Why people use it
- **Bigger returns on limited money,** such as buying a house with a mortgage.
- **Efficiency.** A hedge or exposure can be set up without tying up the full value.
- **Low-risk assets made to earn more.** Some strategies borrow to scale up steady positions. See [[sharpe-ratio]].

## How it works
**Fictional example:** you have 10,000 and borrow another 10,000 to buy 20,000 of shares. That is **2×** leverage.

| Share price move | Position | Owed | Your equity | Change on your money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start | 20,000 | 10,000 | 10,000 | — |
| +25% | 25,000 | 10,000 | 15,000 | **+50%** |
| −25% | 15,000 | 10,000 | 5,000 | **−50%** |
| −50% | 10,000 | 10,000 | 0 | **−100%** |

At 2×, a 50% fall wipes you out. At 3×, a fall of about **33.3%** does. At 10×, just **10%**. Interest on the loan is extra, and lenders usually close the position (a **margin call** or **liquidation**) before your equity reaches zero.

## What goes wrong
- **Forced selling.** Leverage takes away the option to wait. A fall you could have sat through becomes a permanent loss when the lender sells for you.
- **Losses beyond your money.** With some loans and derivatives, you can owe more than you put in.
- **Cascades.** When many leveraged holders are forced to sell at once, their sales push prices down and force more sales.
- **Hidden leverage** inside funds, products and companies you own.

> **Key rule:** leverage turns volatility into survival risk. Ask what fall would wipe you out, and whether that fall has happened before.

## Check yourself

**Q:** With 4× leverage, roughly what fall in the asset wipes out your money?

**A:** 25%. You control four times your money, so a 25% fall on the position equals 100% of your equity.
