## What it is
**Asset allocation** is how you split money across kinds of asset: shares, bonds, cash, property and other real assets. It is usually the biggest single decision in investing, because the mix decides most of how a portfolio behaves: how much it can grow, and how far it can fall.

## Why it matters
Within one asset class, holdings often move broadly together. Between classes, they behave differently: shares grow over time but fall hard in crashes; government bonds are steadier but lose to inflation; cash is safe in money terms but slowly loses buying power. Choosing the mix is choosing which risks to live with.

## How it works
**Start with when you need the money.** Money needed in a year or two shouldn't be in shares, because a crash might not recover in time. Money for thirty years from now can ride out several.

**A classic mix** is **60/40**: 60% in shares for growth, 40% in bonds for stability. Fictional example on 100,000:

| | Start | Shares fall 30%, bonds rise 5% |
|---|---|---|
| Shares | 60,000 | 42,000 |
| Bonds | 40,000 | 42,000 |
| **Total** | **100,000** | **84,000 (−16%)** |

A 30% crash in shares becomes a 16% fall for the whole portfolio, as long as the bonds hold up. When they don't, as in 2022, the cushion is thinner.

**Other inputs** to the mix:
- **Your income.** A safe job lets you take more risk with savings; a volatile one argues for less. See [[human-capital]].
- **Your nerve.** The best mix is one you won't abandon in a crash.
- **Different killers.** The [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]] adds a check: does the mix spread across tiers and kinds of killer, or is everything in tier 3, held through intermediaries?

## What goes wrong
- **Taking more risk than you can hold through a crash,** then selling at the bottom.
- **Home bias:** owning mostly your own country's assets, on top of earning your income there.
- **Ignoring inflation:** a mix that is "safe" in money terms can lose buying power for years.
- **Drift:** without [[rebalancing]], the mix changes by itself as prices move.

> **Key rule:** choose the mix from when you need the money and how much fall you can sit through, then keep to it.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A portfolio is 80% shares and 20% cash. Shares fall 25% and cash is unchanged. How much does the portfolio fall?

**A:** 20%. The shares lose 25% of 80, which is 20, and the cash loses nothing.
