## What it is
**Tax** on investments usually falls on two things:
- **Income:** interest, dividends and rent, often taxed in the year you receive it.
- **Gains:** the rise in value of something you sell, often taxed only when you sell. This is called **capital gains tax** in many countries.

Rules differ widely between countries and change often, so this page explains how tax changes the maths, not the rules where you live.

## Why it matters
Tax is one of the largest costs of investing, and unlike market returns, it can often be reduced or delayed legally. **When** tax is paid matters as much as **how much**, because money not yet paid in tax keeps compounding.

## How it works
**Tax drag, fictional example:** 10,000 invested for 30 years at 7% a year, with a 25% tax rate on gains.

| | How tax is paid | Value after 30 years |
|---|---|---|
| **No tax** | — | 76,123 |
| **Taxed every year** | 25% of each year's gain, so it grows at 5.25% | 46,416 |
| **Taxed once, at the end** | 25% of the total gain on sale | 59,592 |

Same rate, same return. Paying tax once at the end leaves about 13,000 more than paying every year, because the deferred tax kept earning returns. This is why tax-advantaged [[account-types]] are so valuable.

**Other common features:**
- **Losses** can often be set against gains, reducing the tax bill.
- **Holding periods.** Some countries tax assets held longer at lower rates.
- **Crypto** is treated as property in many countries, so each sale or swap can be a taxable event.

## What goes wrong
- **Selling just to rebalance** in a taxable account, creating avoidable tax. Using new money to rebalance avoids it. See [[rebalancing]].
- **Forgetting small events:** swapping one coin for another, or reinvested dividends, can still be taxable.
- **Letting tax drive everything.** Avoiding a sale only to save tax can leave you holding something you no longer want.
- **Poor records,** which make it hard to work out the true cost of what you sold.

> **Key rule:** tax paid later is usually better than tax paid now. Use tax-advantaged accounts first, keep records, and check the rules where you live with a qualified adviser.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why does paying the same tax rate at the end, instead of every year, leave you with more?

**A:** Because the tax you haven't paid yet stays invested and keeps compounding. You pay it eventually, but meanwhile it has earned returns for you.
