## What it is
**Diversification** means spreading your money across holdings that don't all rise and fall together, so that one failure can't sink the whole portfolio.

## Why it works
Every asset carries two kinds of risk:
- **Specific risk:** what can go wrong for that one company or asset: a failed product, a fraud, a fire. Spreading across many holdings dilutes it almost to nothing.
- **Market (systematic) risk:** what hits everything at once: a recession, a jump in interest rates, a financial crisis. No amount of spreading within one market removes it.

Because specific risk can be diversified away cheaply, markets don't reward you for carrying it. Holding one company instead of a hundred adds risk without adding expected return.

## How it works
**Two assets, each with 20% volatility, held 50/50.** The portfolio's volatility depends on how closely they move together ([[correlation]]):

| Correlation | Portfolio volatility |
|---|---|
| +1 (move together exactly) | 20.00% |
| +0.5 | 17.32% |
| 0 (unrelated) | 14.14% |
| −1 (exact opposites) | 0.00% |

The return is the same average in every row; only the swings change. That is the free lunch.

**Many unrelated assets.** With equal amounts in many assets that each swing 20% and are unrelated to each other, the portfolio's swings shrink with the square root of how many there are: 4 assets give 10.00%, 10 give 6.32%, 100 give 2.00%. Real assets are rarely unrelated, so in practice the benefit flattens out sooner.

**Across kinds of risk, not just names.** On the [[cartography-of-wealth|Cartography of Wealth]], the useful spread is across *killers*: theft, the state, counterparties, inflation. Owning several bank deposits spreads across banks but leaves you with one killer.

## What goes wrong
- **Diversifying in name only.** Many funds that own the same large companies are one holding.
- **Crisis correlation.** In a panic, many assets fall together, so the protection is weakest when it's needed most.
- **Diworsification.** Adding assets you don't understand, or that cost a lot, can raise costs without lowering risk.

> **Key rule:** diversify across things that fail for different reasons. Count the killers, not the holdings.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why doesn't owning every share in the market remove all risk?

**A:** Because market risk hits all companies together. Spreading across companies removes each one's specific risk, but a recession or crash still affects the whole market.
