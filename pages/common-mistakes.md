## What it is
A short list of the errors that cost ordinary investors the most. Most of them aren't about choosing the wrong asset. They're about behaviour, costs and risk.

## Why it matters
Avoiding a big mistake usually does more for long-term results than finding a great investment. Losses compound too: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover. See [[drawdown]].

## How it works
**1. Buying high, selling low.** People pile in after a rise and sell after a fall. Morningstar estimates that over the ten years to 2023 the average dollar invested in US funds earned 1.1% a year less than the funds themselves, about 6.3% a year against 7.4%, because of mistimed purchases and sales.

**2. Paying too much.** High fund fees, frequent trading and hidden charges compound against you every year. See [[funds-and-etfs]].

**3. Concentration.** Too much in one company, one sector, your employer, or one coin. See [[position-sizing]].

**4. Leverage you can't survive.** Borrowing turns a temporary fall into a forced sale. See [[leverage]].

**5. No emergency fund.** Without cash set aside, an unexpected bill forces you to sell investments at whatever price the market offers.

**6. Chasing yields and promises.** A return far above what safe assets pay is a sign of risk, or of fraud.

**7. Scams.** Guaranteed returns, pressure to act fast, payment in crypto or by transfer to a stranger, and "recovery" services that charge to get back money already lost are common warning signs.

**8. Ignoring free money and tax breaks,** such as an employer's matched contribution. See [[account-types]].

## What goes wrong
The common thread: decisions made on feelings in the moment. Fear sells at the bottom; excitement buys at the top; overconfidence concentrates and borrows.

> **Key rule:** set your rules in calm times (how much to save, what mix, when to rebalance) and follow them when you feel least like it.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why can investors earn less than the funds they own?

**A:** Because they move money in and out at the wrong times: adding after strong performance and pulling out after falls. The fund's return assumes you held it the whole time.
