## What it is
A **memecoin** is a token built around a joke, a mascot or an online community, with no claim on anything and no cash flows. Dogecoin, created in 2013 as a joke, is the best-known early example; it has no fixed supply cap.

## Why it matters
Memecoins are the purest test of the question "is it backed?" — they aren't, by anything. Their price is set entirely by how many people want to hold them. That makes them a clear lens on attention, liquidity and crowd behaviour.

## How it works
- **Easy to create:** anyone can launch a token in minutes, so thousands appear.
- **Price is attention:** social media, celebrity mentions and listings on large exchanges drive demand.
- **Liquidity is thin:** most memecoins trade in small pools, so a few large sellers can crash the price.
- **Liquidity cycles:** speculative tokens tend to rise most when [[liquidity]] is plentiful and fall hardest when it drains.

## What goes wrong
- **Rug pulls:** the creators sell their large holdings or withdraw the trading pool's funds.
- **Pump and dump:** coordinated buying to lift a price, followed by insiders selling.
- **Insider supply:** a small group often holds most of the tokens from the start.
- **Fading interest:** when attention moves on, there is nothing underneath.

> **Key rule:** a memecoin's only support is other people wanting it tomorrow. Size any holding as if it could go to zero.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A memecoin's quoted price is 1 dollar and you hold a million tokens. Do you have a million dollars?

**A:** Not necessarily. If the trading pool is small, selling even a fraction would push the price down sharply. A holding is only worth what you can sell it for.
