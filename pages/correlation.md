## What it is
**Correlation** measures how closely two things move together, on a scale from −1 to +1:
- **+1:** they always move in the same direction, in proportion.
- **0:** knowing one's move tells you nothing about the other's.
- **−1:** they always move in opposite directions.

It's calculated from past returns, usually monthly or daily, over a chosen period.

## Why it matters
Correlation is the input that makes [[diversification]] work. Two assets with high returns and low correlation together can be steadier than either alone. Two assets with high correlation are, for risk purposes, close to the same asset.

## How it works
| Correlation | Reads as | Example of the kind of pair |
|---|---|---|
| Close to +1 | Move as one | Two large-company index funds |
| Around +0.5 | Often together | Shares in different countries |
| Around 0 | Unrelated | Shares and some commodities, in some periods |
| Negative | Tend to offset | Shares and government bonds, in many past decades |

**It only measures straight-line, average behaviour.** A correlation near zero can still hide two assets that fall together in rare crashes and drift apart the rest of the time.

## What goes wrong
- **It changes.** Correlations measured in calm years can jump towards +1 in a crisis, when investors sell everything to raise cash. See [[market-regimes]].
- **The stock–bond pairing can flip.** For much of the 2000s and 2010s, government bonds tended to rise when shares fell. In 2022, with inflation high and interest rates rising, US shares fell about 18% and US bonds about 13% in the same year.
- **Correlation isn't cause.** Two things moving together may share a hidden driver, or may line up by chance.
- **Too short a history** gives a number that looks precise but isn't.

> **Key rule:** don't trust the average correlation. Ask what each holding did in the last crisis, and what would make them fall together.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Two funds have a correlation of 0.95. You own both. How diversified are you?

**A:** Hardly at all. They move almost as one, so owning both is close to owning twice as much of one.
