## What it is
A bank has two separate defences, and failing at either one can bring it down:
- **Capital** — the owners' money in the bank (equity). It absorbs losses before depositors lose anything. Too little capital and the bank is **insolvent**: it owes more than it owns.
- **Liquidity** — assets that can be turned into cash quickly without big losses. Too little liquidity and the bank is **illiquid**: solvent on paper, but unable to pay everyone who asks today.

## Why it matters
Banks fund long-term, hard-to-sell loans with deposits that can leave at any moment. That mismatch is what makes banking useful, and what makes runs possible. Capital and liquidity are how a bank survives bad loans and nervous depositors.

## How it works
| Measure | What it compares | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital ratio** | Capital against the bank's assets, usually weighted by how risky they are | How big a loss the bank can take before depositors are at risk |
| **Leverage ratio** | Capital against total assets, with no risk weighting | A simple check that can't be gamed with low risk weights |
| **Liquidity coverage** | Liquid assets against the cash that could flow out in a 30-day stress | Whether the bank can survive a month-long run |
| **Loan-to-deposit ratio** | Loans against deposits | How much of the deposit base is tied up in loans |

These rules come from the international **Basel** standards, which most major economies apply in their own versions.

**Worked example (fictional figures):** a bank has assets of 1,000 and capital of 50, so its simple leverage ratio is 5%. If loans worth 60 go bad, the losses exceed its capital, and depositors' money is no longer fully covered.

## What goes wrong
- **Losses hidden by accounting.** Bonds a bank says it will hold to maturity don't have to be marked at market prices, so losses on them can stay out of sight until the bank is forced to sell. This was central to Silicon Valley Bank's collapse in 2023.
- **Fast runs.** Online banking and social media let depositors leave in hours, faster than rules built around 30 days assume.
- **Concentrated depositors.** A bank whose deposits come from a few large, connected customers can lose them all at once.

> **Key rule:** a bank fails in one of two ways — it runs out of capital (insolvent) or runs out of cash (illiquid). Check both.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A bank owns more than it owes, but can't pay all the depositors asking for money today. Is it insolvent or illiquid?

**A:** Illiquid. It has enough value in total, but not enough cash right now. This is the situation a central bank's emergency lending is designed for. See [[lender-of-last-resort]].
