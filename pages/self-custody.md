## What it is
**Self-custody** means holding the keys to your own coins in a wallet only you control. See [[keys-and-wallets]] for how keys work.

## Why people choose it
- **No custodian risk:** an exchange failing can't take your coins.
- **No freezes:** nobody can block your coins without your keys.
- **Privacy:** the coins aren't held in an account under your name.

It is the digital version of keeping gold in your own safe: you remove the middleman, and become the security team.

## How to do it well
- **Hardware wallet** for any meaningful amount.
- **Seed phrase backed up offline**, in more than one secure place, never photographed or typed into a website.
- **Test a recovery** with a small amount before you rely on it.
- **Multisig** for large amounts, so one lost or stolen key isn't fatal.
- **An inheritance plan** that lets someone you trust recover the coins, without making them easy to steal.

## What goes wrong
- **Lost seed phrase:** coins locked forever.
- **Leaked seed phrase:** coins stolen, with no recourse.
- **Phishing and malicious approvals.** See [[keys-and-wallets]].
- **Protocol failure:** self-custody protects you from firms failing, not from the chain or token failing.
- **Physical threats:** people known to hold crypto can be targeted directly.

> **Key rule:** self-custody swaps counterparty risk for your own operational risk. Only do it with a backup you have tested.

## Check yourself

**Q:** What does self-custody protect you from, and what doesn't it?

**A:** It protects you from a custodian failing, freezing or being hacked. It doesn't protect you from losing your keys, being tricked into signing, or the coin itself failing.
