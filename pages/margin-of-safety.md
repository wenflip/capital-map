## What it is
A margin of safety is the gap between the price you pay and a conservative estimate of what something is worth. The larger the gap, the more the estimate can be wrong before the decision loses money.

## Why it matters
Estimates of value are uncertain. Cash flows, growth and discount rates can all be misjudged. A decision that only works if every estimate is exactly right has no room for error. A margin of safety absorbs some of that error.

## How it works
Estimate value using cautious assumptions. Then require the price to be sufficiently below that estimate before acting. The required gap depends on how uncertain the estimate is and how severe the loss would be if you are wrong. The margin does not guarantee a good outcome. It only raises the chance that ordinary errors in the estimate do not turn the decision into a loss.

**Worked example (fictional figures):** a conservative estimate of value is 100. You require a 30% margin, so you will pay at most 70. If the true value turns out to be 85 (15% below your estimate), paying 70 still leaves 85 − 70 = **15** of value. Paying the full 100 would have meant 85 − 100 = **−15**: the same error, now a loss.

## What goes wrong
- **Using an optimistic estimate.** The margin is only as good as the conservatism of the starting value.
- **Treating the margin as a target return.** It is a buffer against error, not a forecast of profit.
- **Ignoring it when the story is strong.** The more confident the narrative, the more useful the buffer becomes.

> **Key rule:** require a gap between price and a cautious estimate of value so that ordinary errors do not destroy the decision.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Your estimate of value is 50 and the price is 48. Is there a useful margin of safety?

**A:** A 4% gap (48 / 50 = 0.96) is small. If the estimate is uncertain by more than that, the margin is not large enough to absorb ordinary error.

**Q:** You lower your required margin because recent results have been good. What risk does that create?

**A:** The buffer shrinks at the same time confidence rises. Errors that the original margin would have absorbed can now produce losses.
