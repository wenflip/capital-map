## What it is
**Custodial crypto** is crypto held for you by a firm: an exchange account, a broker, or a fund that holds coins. The firm controls the keys. What you own is the firm's promise to give you the coins.

## Why people use it
- **Convenience:** buy, sell and move between currency and crypto in one place.
- **No key management:** no seed phrase to lose.
- **Familiar wrappers:** some markets offer funds that hold crypto and trade like shares, through an ordinary brokerage account.

## How it works
In the [[claims-and-liabilities|claims]] sense, a custodial balance is the firm's liability to you. What protects it depends on the firm and where it operates:
- whether customer coins are kept **separate** from the firm's own;
- whether the firm publishes **proof of reserves** (evidence it holds what it owes);
- how a **bankruptcy** would treat customers.

## What goes wrong
- **Exchange collapse.** In November 2022 the exchange FTX collapsed after customer funds were found to have been misused, and customers became creditors in a long bankruptcy. Mt. Gox, once the largest Bitcoin exchange, failed in 2014 after losing customers' coins.
- **Freezes:** withdrawals can be halted in a crisis.
- **Hacks** of the exchange's own wallets.
- **Rehypothecation:** some platforms lend out customer coins to earn income, which adds a second layer of risk.

> **Key rule:** "not your keys, not your coins". A custodial balance is a claim, so judge the firm the way you would judge a bank.

## Check yourself

**Q:** Why is a custodial balance closer to a bank deposit than to coins in your own wallet?

**A:** Because in both cases you hold a claim on a firm that holds the asset. Your money is only as safe as the firm and the rules protecting customers.
