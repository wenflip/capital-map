## What it is
Two different forces set a price:
- **Fundamentals:** what an asset is worth, judged by the income it can produce over its life. See [[valuation]].
- **Flows:** the money that has to go in or out at a given moment, whatever the asset is worth.

Over many years, fundamentals tend to win. Over days and months, flows often do.

## Why it matters
A lot of buying and selling has nothing to do with anyone's view of value. Index funds buy what's in the index. Pension funds rebalance on set dates. Companies buy back their own shares. Funds sell when their investors ask for their money back. Knowing where the flows come from explains moves that the news can't.

## How it works
**Where big flows come from.**
- **Index changes.** When a company joins a major index, funds that track the index must buy it, and those dropping out must be sold. See [[indices]] and [[funds-and-etfs]].
- **Rebalancing.** Funds with fixed mixes sell what has risen and buy what has fallen to get back to target, often at month or quarter end. See [[rebalancing]].
- **Buybacks.** Companies buying their own shares are a steady buyer that doesn't care about the day's price.
- **Investors arriving or leaving.** Money pouring into or out of a type of fund has to be invested or raised, whatever the prices.

**A real case.** When it was announced on 16 November 2020 that Tesla would join the S&P 500, its shares rose more than 13% in trading after the market closed. Funds tracking the index would have to buy the stock when it joined, before trading on 21 December 2020.

**Fictional example: how big a flow can be.** A company has 200 million worth of shares that are freely traded. Joining an index means tracking funds must buy 50 million worth. That's **25%** of all the shares available to buy, all wanted at once.

## What goes wrong
- **Mistaking a flow for a verdict.** A price pushed up by forced buying hasn't been judged more valuable. When the flow stops, the price can drift back.
- **Flows reverse.** Money that rushed in can rush out for the same mechanical reasons. See [[forced-sellers]].
- **Crowding.** When many funds follow the same rules, they all trade the same way at the same time.

> **Key rule:** before reading a price move as news about value, ask who had to buy or sell, and whether they'll have to do it again.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A company has 400 million of freely traded shares. Index funds must buy 60 million worth. What share of the tradable shares is that?

**A:** 60 ÷ 400 = **15%** of the shares available to buy.
