## What it is
A **drawdown** is the fall from a previous peak to a later low, measured as a percentage of the peak. The **maximum drawdown** is the worst such fall over a period. It answers the question most investors actually care about: how bad did it get?

## Why it matters
Drawdowns are what make people abandon their plans. A portfolio that falls by half tests anyone's nerve, and anyone who sells near the bottom turns a temporary fall into a permanent loss. Knowing the likely depth of a drawdown in advance is how you choose a mix you can hold. Large drawdowns are also hard to recover from: a 50% fall needs a 100% gain to get back. See [[position-sizing]].

## How it works
Fictional example of a portfolio's value at the end of each year:

| Year | Value | Peak so far | Drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 100 | 0% |
| 1 | 120 | 120 | 0% |
| 2 | 90 | 120 | −25% |
| 3 | 110 | 120 | −8.3% |
| 4 | 130 | 130 | 0% |

The maximum drawdown is **−25%**, from 120 to 90. The portfolio was **underwater**, below its previous peak, for three years, until it passed 120.

**A real one.** The S&P 500 fell about 57% from its October 2007 closing high to its March 2009 low, and didn't close above that 2007 high again until March 2013, five and a half years later (before dividends).

## What goes wrong
- **History understates the future.** The worst drawdown so far is not the worst possible. A short record may not include a real crisis.
- **Time matters as much as depth.** Years spent underwater can be harder to bear than a quick, deep fall.
- **Measured at the wrong frequency.** Year-end values can hide a much deeper fall within the year.
- **Leverage** turns drawdowns into margin calls and forced sales, so the loss becomes permanent. See [[leverage]].

> **Key rule:** before you invest, ask how big a fall you could sit through without selling, and choose a mix whose history suggests something smaller.

## Check yourself

**Q:** A fund rises from 100 to 200, falls to 140, then rises to 180. What is its maximum drawdown so far?

**A:** −30%, from the peak of 200 to the low of 140. It is still underwater, because 180 is below 200.
